What real sign-in screens actually look like

Live screenshots taken just now, unedited, of the actual sign-in pages of products widely regarded as well designed. Nothing here was mocked up or invented; every image is that product's real page at the URL underneath it.

The pattern, across all nine: Not one of them puts a logo wall, trust bar, or any animated/glass/gradient decoration on the sign-in screen itself. The form sits alone on a plain background, small brand mark, nothing competing with it. The one product here that does show customer logos near a sign-in flow (Retool) puts them in a completely separate zone, below an illustration, as flat black wordmarks with zero color, zero motion, zero styling — not the hero of the screen, a quiet footnote to it.

Sign-in screens with zero decoration

Notion

notion.so/login

Wordmark, heading, one input, provider grid. Nothing else.

Vercel

vercel.com/login

Plain grey ground, stacked provider buttons, no imagery at all.

GitHub

github.com/login

The oldest, most-used dev sign-in on the web. Still just a mark and a form.

Figma

figma.com/login

Logo top-left, form dead centre, white space everywhere else.

Attio

app.attio.com/login

Widely cited as one of the best-designed B2B products right now. One button, one field.

Linear

linear.app/login

The reference for restraint in this category. A mark, a heading, four buttons.

Mercury

mercury.com/login

A banking product; if anyone needed to look "trustworthy" via logos, it's them. They didn't.

Stripe

dashboard.stripe.com/login

One static brand gradient behind a plain white card. Compare to their own homepage below.

The one exception, and how it actually handles it

Retool

login.retool.com

The only one of the nine with any customer logos near a sign-in flow. Look at how it's done: flat black wordmarks (Ramp, DoorDash, Stripe, Amazon, Adobe, OpenAI), no colour, no icons, no motion, in their own quiet strip under an illustration — nowhere near the form, which still has zero decoration of its own.
